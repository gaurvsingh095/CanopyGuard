    What these tests do prove

    The current deterministic rules behave as specified on these 40 inputs: inversion detection, wind-boundary checks, shear thresholds, wrap-around direction math, stale/invalid inputs and volatility cautions. Regression tests are reproducible and downloadable.

    What they do not prove

    They are engineered synthetic labels produced for rule testing, not independently annotated agronomic observations. They cannot establish field accuracy, false-positive rates, chemical deposition, drift transport, or regulatory compliance. Field validation needs calibrated sensor towers, weather logs and specialist oversight.

    Threshold transparency

    No black-box score, no fabricated regulatory limits

    STOP if any valid measured upper temperature is warmer than the lower temperature, wind at either height is outside your configured limits, or either reading is missing/stale. Severe shear also generates a conservative STOP in this prototype.

    CAUTION for moderate vertical wind shear or elevated product-volatility concerns. GO means no configured weather trigger fired; it never authorizes application.

    Illustrative wind-shear defaults: 4 mph or 45° → CAUTION; 6 mph or 90° → STOP. These are demo alert settings, not validated legal or scientific thresholds.

    METHODS / TRANSPARENCY

    Designed to explain, not overpromise.

    Temperature inversion

    Set ΔT = T(30 ft) − T(5 ft). When ΔT is positive, the upper level is warmer: that meets the challenge definition of a temperature inversion. An inversion is grounds for STOP. Real instruments require calibration, quality checks and measurement uncertainty handling.

    Wind screening

    Check wind speed at both heights against configurable limits, initially 3–10 mph per UC IPM general guidance. Compute absolute speed difference and shortest circular direction difference (min(|a−b|,360−|a−b|)). Direction change thresholds are prototype-specific heuristics and should be validated with agricultural meteorology experts.

    Source credibility

    On-site sensors are the primary evidence. Regional API forecasts can guide the timing of a *future on-site check*, but their altitude, terrain and model grid do not establish a canopy-layer inversion. Demonstration values are simulated, not real orchard observations.

    Safety and legal limitation

    This is a hackathon decision-support prototype, not an approved spray-control system. It does not determine legal compliance or guarantee zero drift. Always follow product labels, assess sensitive areas and buffer zones, confirm calibrated equipment and inspect field conditions. Do not spray based solely on a GO indication.
